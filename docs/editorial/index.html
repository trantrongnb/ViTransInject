Abstract

Vietnamese LLM agents may normalize or translate untrusted content before processing, yet it remains unclear how such transformations affect susceptibility to indirect prompt injection. We present ViTransInject-Bench, a Vietnamese benchmark covering multiple agent tasks, linguistic representations, attack strategies, and translation-mediated deployment pipelines. We introduce the Translation Amplification Factor to quantify paired changes in attack success across linguistic representations, and evaluate a Cross-View Consistency Guard that compares agent behavior across original Vietnamese, diacritic-free Vietnamese, and English-translated views.

Across five open-weight models, bilingual deployment reaches a mean ASR of 9.7%, compared with 7.1% under direct Vietnamese processing. Machine-translated and bilingual configurations obtain pooled TAF values of 1.30 and 1.27, although the direction and magnitude of these effects vary across models. The guard reduces mean ASR from 7.7% to 3.6% while producing fewer false positives on clean look-alike cases than keyword filtering, at the cost of additional agent inference.

1Overview

Large language model agents increasingly process external content such as emails, retrieved documents, customer records, and search results, and may act on this content through tools. Untrusted content can contain instructions that redirect the agent from the user's intended task. This threat, indirect prompt injection (IPI), can lead to task hijacking, unintended tool use, or information disclosure.

The same document can reach the agent as original Vietnamese, as diacritic-free text, as teencode, as a machine translation, or as a bilingual pair. Each form can change what the injected instruction does.

Existing IPI benchmarks primarily evaluate English-language agent settings. Multilingual safety studies show that model behavior varies across languages, but they do not examine the case in which an injection is already embedded in a Vietnamese document and the language transformation is introduced by the deployment pipeline. ViTransInject studies exactly that setting. The main contributions are:

  1. ViTransInject-Bench, a Vietnamese indirect prompt injection benchmark covering five agent tasks, six linguistic representations, five injection styles, and three attacker goals, together with clean and clean look-alike cases.
  2. The Translation Amplification Factor (TAF) and a paired evaluation protocol for measuring changes in attack success across deployment pipelines and linguistic representations, complemented by absolute ASR change and safe-to-attacked flip rates.
  3. A Cross-View Consistency Guard that detects behavioral disagreement across original Vietnamese, diacritic-free Vietnamese, and English-translated views using deterministic action signatures.

2Benchmark

Each task consists of a trusted user request and untrusted content such as an email, a retrieved document, or a search result. The attacker may modify only the untrusted content by inserting a malicious span; it cannot alter the request or the system prompt. Every case defines a machine-checkable success predicate, and the agent operates in a single turn, returning either a structured tool call or a text answer. Tools are simulated: names and arguments are recorded but nothing is executed.

Source cases

ViTransInject-Bench starts from 250 synthetic task seeds, 50 for each of five task types: email handling, retrieval-augmented question answering, customer support, summarization, and form extraction. Each seed holds a trusted request, an untrusted document, a benign task goal, and a reference answer. A further 100 clean look-alike seeds contain instruction-related expressions such as ignore and system in legitimate contexts and are used to measure false positives.

Linguistic representations

Every clean and attack record is rendered into six representations while preserving its benign goal and success condition:

vi_full
The original Vietnamese document.
vi_nodiacritic
Diacritics removed through Unicode decomposition and deterministic character mapping.
teencode
A chat-style abbreviated Vietnamese representation.
mt_en
An English machine translation of the document (Google Translate, no post-editing).
bilingual
The original Vietnamese document concatenated with its English translation.
mixed
A Vietnamese–English code-mixed representation at the word or sentence level.

3Method

Deployment pipelines

Four configurations vary how the trusted request, the untrusted document, and the system prompt are presented to the agent. All start from vi_full, and the external translation engine is separate from the evaluated model.

P1 · Vietnamese direct
Document, request, and system prompt remain in Vietnamese.
P2 · Full translation
Document and request are translated into English and paired with an English system prompt.
P3 · Query-only
Only the request is translated, with an English system prompt; the document stays in Vietnamese.
P4 · Bilingual context
The Vietnamese document is concatenated with its English translation; request and system prompt stay in Vietnamese.

Translation Amplification Factor

Let ASRv denote the attack success rate under representation v. TAF measures the relative change in attack success with respect to the original Vietnamese representation:

TAFv =ASRvASRvi_full(5)

A value above 1 indicates a higher ASR than vi_full; below 1, a lower one. Because a ratio can look large when the baseline is small, the absolute change ΔASRv = ASRv − ASRvi_full is also reported, together with the safe-to-attacked flip rate: the share of paired attack cases that fail under vi_full but succeed under v.

Experimental setup

Five self-hosted, open-weight instruction-tuned models between 1B and 3B parameters are evaluated: Llama-3.2-1B, Qwen2.5-1.5B, Qwen2.5-3B, Gemma-2-2B, and Sailor2-1B. Decoding temperature is 0. All experiments use the test split (1,200 attack, 1,200 clean, 80 clean look-alike records). Reported metrics are Attack Success Rate (ASR), Benign Task Utility (BTU), False Positive Rate (FPR), Detection Rate (DR), TAF, absolute ASR change, and flip rate.

4Results

RQ1 · Attack success across deployment pipelines

Attack success varies across pipelines, but no pipeline produces a consistent effect across all models. Mean ASR rises from 0.071 under direct Vietnamese processing (P1) to 0.097 under bilingual context (P4), driven mainly by Qwen2.5-3B and Sailor2-1B. Gemma-2-2B remains comparatively stable and is slightly lower under the translation-mediated pipelines.

RQ2 · Effects of linguistic representations

The cross-lingual configurations produce the largest pooled increases. mt_en and bilingual raise pooled ASR from 0.071 to 0.092 and 0.090, corresponding to TAF values of 1.30 and 1.27. Both increase ASR for four of five models and show the highest flip rates. Teencode yields the lowest pooled ASR and does not increase attack success for any model.

5Defense

The Cross-View Consistency Guard evaluates the same document in three views (original Vietnamese, diacritic-free Vietnamese, and English translation) with the task, model, tools, and decoding fixed. Each parsed output is mapped to a deterministic action signature: ANSWER when no tool is called, or tool:target where the target is the normalized security-relevant argument, such as the lowercase recipient of send_email. The case is blocked when the three signatures disagree.

Because the guard compares actions rather than wording, differences in output text or language alone never trigger a block. It can therefore miss attacks that remain text-only or that induce the same action in every view. It requires three agent passes per case.

Guard FPR ranges from 0% (Gemma-2-2B) to 35% (Llama-3.2-1B, Sailor2-1B). The largest BTU drop under the guard is Sailor2-1B, from 69.9% to 47.2%.

The guard obtains the lowest mean residual ASR and is best for Llama-3.2-1B, Qwen2.5-1.5B, and Gemma-2-2B, whereas keyword filtering is lower for Qwen2.5-3B and Sailor2-1B. Keyword filtering blocks every clean look-alike case. Spotlighting preserves the most benign utility and incurs no blocking false positives, but reduces attack success less than the guard.
