ViTransInject: Evaluating Translation-Mediated Indirect Prompt Injection in Vietnamese LLM Agents

Abstract, condensed

ViTransInject-Bench renders 250 Vietnamese task seeds into six linguistic representations, each keeping one machine-checkable success condition. The Translation Amplification Factor (TAF) measures paired change in attack success. A Cross-View Consistency Guard compares the agent's action across three language views and blocks on disagreement.

Five open-weight 1–3B models: bilingual deployment reaches mean ASR 9.7% versus 7.1% for direct Vietnamese; mt_en and bilingual pool to TAF 1.30 and 1.27; the guard cuts mean ASR from 7.7% to 3.6% at three agent passes per case.

1

Every dossier is built from one seed and stays paired.

A seed holds a trusted request, an untrusted document, a benign goal, and a reference answer. Its clean record keeps the document as is; its attack twin carries an inserted span. Both are rendered into six representations that share the same success condition, so any change in outcome is attributable to the form of the text.

Styles: plain imperative, authority spoofing, camouflage blending, instruction with justification, obfuscated or indirect instruction. Five task types: email handling, retrieval-augmented question answering, customer support, summarization, form extraction. The span is inserted at the beginning, middle, or end of the untrusted document only.
Threat model

The attacker edits only the untrusted content, never the request or the system prompt. The agent runs one turn and returns either a structured tool call or a text answer. Tools are simulated: names and arguments are recorded, nothing executes. An attack succeeds when the case's predicate holds on the parsed output, for example a tool call redirected to an attacker-controlled address.

2

Six forms of the same text. The amplification is measured pairwise.

Let ASRv be the attack success rate under representation v. TAFv = ASRv / ASRvi_full. Because a ratio inflates on a small baseline, the absolute change ΔASR and the safe-to-attacked flip rate, the share of paired attacks that fail in Vietnamese but succeed in form v, are reported with it. Try the tabs on the dossier above: the amplification stamp reissues for each form.

Inspector's note

No pipeline is uniformly worse. Query-only translation is the worst case for Qwen2.5-1.5B, bilingual context for Qwen2.5-3B and Sailor2-1B, and Gemma-2-2B is slightly safer under every translation-mediated pipeline. Sailor2-1B doubles from 0.065 to 0.130 when the English translation is appended. Any deployment claim about translation safety has to name the model.

4

Three inspectors read three forms. Disagreement is refused.

The Cross-View Consistency Guard runs the same case as original Vietnamese, diacritic-free Vietnamese, and English translation, with the model, tools, and decoding fixed. Each output is reduced to an action signature: ANSWER when no tool is called, otherwise tool:target with the security-relevant argument normalized. Three matching signatures admit the case. Anything else is blocked. Wording and output language never count.

Trade-off

Lowest residual attack success, highest cost. The guard is best for Llama-3.2-1B, Qwen2.5-1.5B, and Gemma-2-2B; keyword filtering edges it for Qwen2.5-3B and Sailor2-1B but refuses every clean look-alike. Spotlighting keeps the most benign utility with no blocking false positives and removes the least attack success. The guard pays three agent passes per case and a model-dependent false-positive rate.

Released to the public record.

Benchmark, evaluation harness, simulated tools, and defense configurations are public. Scope of the findings: single-turn agent, simulated tools, temperature 0, open-weight models of 1–3B parameters; transfer to multi-step agents or larger models is not claimed.
